## Tuning numbers
- **Health orb:**
  - drops on 18% of minion kills, or 34% when the player is below 40% HP;
  - a pity timer guarantees one on the 7th kill in a row without a drop;
  - heals 12% of max HP.
- **Shard coin:** 8% chance when no orb dropped, worth +1 each.
- **Full HP rule:** an orb is not collected and ignores the magnet at full HP; it waits on the floor until the player needs it.
- **Placement:** it lands 0.8–1.8 m from the dead minion, clamped to the arena and outside `Arena.Obstacles`, after a 0.5 s arc.
- **Lifetime:** 14 s, blinking in the last 3 s (faster in the last 1.5 s). The timer only runs while `ctx.Running`, so pausing stops it.
- **Magnet and collect:** pull starts within 3.5 m and speeds up from 3 to 16 m/s on a quadratic ramp. Collect happens at 0.8 m.
- **Collect effect:** sparkles, a ring and a floating "+N". The "+N" is hidden when the `damageNumbers` setting is off.

- **Core** (`Core/Speech.cs`, no scene code):

<result>The proposal is written and committed as `3836b95` on `feature/boss-duel`, with an index line added to `docs/README.md`. Nothing was merged or pushed. It is a design document only, with no gameplay code. All timings, weights and payoff numbers in it are starting values that bot runs still need to replace.

**Two bugs found in the current Feint move** (`BossMoves.cs:115-118`):
- The real swing has a 0.4 s warning. That is below the 0.45 s minimum, because `Boss.cs:110` only enforces the minimum on the first, fake part.
- The real swing never marks its warning, so `TelegraphFrac` reads -1 during it. Dodges against it are left out of the early/late counts, which skews `EarlyRatio`, the stat the boss learns from. The proposal says to fix this first.

**What the code already shows:**
- The boss is not really an HP sponge. It has 320 HP against about 26 player DPS. What it lacks is any way to be interrupted (`ReactsToHits =&gt; false`), and the player has no way to open a damage window.
- Nothing can target the companion. It is a plain `MonoBehaviour` with no HP. The boss's damage checks (`BossMove.cs:59-71`), minions (`Enemy.cs:29`) and hostile projectiles (`Projectile.cs:79-85`) all check only the player.

**Summary of the design (the doc opens with a 10-line version):**
1. **Boss wins by decisions, not numbers.** About 300 HP against a team of player 100 plus companion 70–140. No single hit takes more than 35% of a target's max HP. Phase 2 stops shortening warnings.
2. **Every attack has a commit point.** The windup before it can be cancelled or changed. After it there are always at least 450 ms before the hit, and the cue is the same for every move (red flash plus sound). Everything before the flash may be a lie; everything after is true.
3. **Tricks cost a visible resource.** The boss has Focus, shown on the HUD. Feints, delays, a guard-counter stance and cancelling its own recovery all cost Focus. Players can break the boss's Poise to open big damage windows.
4. **In-fight decisions are a small game.** The boss tracks how the player usually reacts to each move type, mixes a safe randomised strategy with an exploiting one (capped), and always keeps at least 35% honest attacks.
5. **The companion is a real partner.** Pairing A (player warrior + companion mage) is the first version; pairing B (player mage + companion warrior) comes later. Combos include taunt windows, a frost mark the other partner shatters, back-attack bonuses, and the mage setting off the boss's counter stance from range.
6. **Boss and minions choose targets** using a few public, explainable factors, with stickiness before switching, a visible switch cue, and limits on how often the boss can hunt the companion. A downed companion can be revived and never dies permanently.
7. **The LLM never acts mid-fight.** Between fights it sets bounded personality knobs and writes taunts that quote measured habits. Measured facts stay with the rule-based code.
8. **Plan:** four milestones, M0–M3, about 2.5–3 weeks, each with bot-measurable targets.

**Top 5 design decisions and why:**
1. **The commit-point contract with a 450 ms floor.** It makes "the boss tricked me" fair. Fighting games build mind games around deliberate gaps, and two-choice human reaction averages 350–450 ms. The 450 ms floor alone does not create the mind game. Players cannot dash during their own attack windup (`ActionTimer.cs:33`), so attacking during a boss windup cuts into their own reaction time. That makes greed a real gamble with no hidden rules.
2. **Boss Focus is visible.** Sirlin's point about Yomi is that you can only read an opponent when payoffs are unequal and you can judge their resources. A visible bar also means that when Focus is empty, every attack is honest, which is a guarantee players can learn.
3. **Mix a safe strategy with exploitation, capped at 50%, plus an honest floor.** Poker research (restricted Nash response) shows pure counter-play is itself easy to exploit. I solved the example payoff matrix with regret matching:
   - The safe policy guarantees the boss about +0.2 per exchange.
   - Against a habitual early dodger, exploitation raises the boss's expected value from 2.93 to 4.13.
   - A player who switches to waiting for the flash flips it to −2.05.

   So changing your habit beats the learning boss, which gives the cross-run "he learns, I adapt" loop the user asked for. Feints only pay off against early dodgers, which gives the taunts an honest reason.
4. **Poise and break windows replace a big HP pool.** This follows Sekiro's posture system: about 40% of boss damage is meant to land during breaks, so damage output comes from decisions.
5. **The companion can go down but not die, under fairness limits.** Elizabeth (BioShock Infinite) and Ellie (The Last of Us) were made immune to avoid escort frustration. The user explicitly wants the companion to be attackable, so the doc adds safeguards instead: target stickiness modelled on WoW's 110%/130% threat thresholds, visible target marking, limits on hunting the companion, the companion auto-dodging, and revive as a risk/reward choice.
