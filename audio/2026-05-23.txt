# English Learning Article: Asking Technical Questions and Describing Actions

In today's conversation, we used several common English patterns for asking about system status and giving instructions. Let's look at some key vocabulary and grammar.

## Vocabulary to Learn

.  activate / enable – to turn on or make something work
  - "Is it activated now?" / "I need to enable the coach."
.  input / output – what you enter (input) and what the system returns (output)
  - "The original input is passed to the agent." / "I don't see the corrected output."
.  pass to – to send or forward something
  - "You pass the original sentence to the agent."
.  coach / correct – to fix or improve something
  - "The coach corrects my English sentences."

## Grammar Focus: Present Simple Questions and Answers

When checking if a feature works, we often use present simple questions:

.  "Does the hook activate now?" → Yes, it does. / No, it doesn't.
.  "How do I test it?" → You open a new terminal.

We also use past simple for experiences:

.  "I didn't see the coach hook output."
.  "It auto-started."

## Practical Tip

When you don't understand something, ask for details:

.  "Can you show me the right sentence?"
.  "What functionality should I add?"

Be direct but polite. Use "do / does" to form questions, and keep your sentences short and clear.
