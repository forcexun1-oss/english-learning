## Slab-Size Sweep Results (warm mmap, `LLAMA_EXPERT_CACHE_SHARED_EVENT_DISABLE=1` for ≥8 GB points)

All ≥8 GB points here used `LLAMA_EXPERT_CACHE_SHARED_EVENT_DISABLE=1` so numbers are comparable to the 4 GB sync baseline. Timeline columns are per-layer averages × 40 MoE layers to give per-token totals.

| slab GB | steady-state tok/s | hit-rate | wait-pread ms/tok | compute-residual ms/tok | pread bandwidth MiB/ms | peak compressor GB |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **4** | **21.9** | 55.90% | 18.48 | 27.04 | 18.87 | 5.8 |
| **8** | **23.3** | 72.43% | 15.96 | 27.00 | 13.16 | 5.8 |
| **12** | **22.8** | 80.58% | 16.36 | 27.28 | 9.21 | 5.5 |

No points were skipped for memory safety. Peak stored compressor rose from 5.8 → 6.4 GB over the sweep — well under the 20.7 GB kill threshold throughout.

---

### Key observations

**pread bandwidth degrades inversely with slab size**: 18.87 → 8.72 MiB/ms as the slab grows 4× from 4 to 16 GB. Larger MTLBuffers have materially slower DMA throughput, which partially cancels the benefit of fewer cache misses. At 12 GB the net pread time actually ticked UP slightly vs 8 GB (16.36 vs 15.96 ms/tok) before dropping again at 16 GB.

**compute/sync floor is immovable**: 26.88–27.28 ms/tok across all slab sizes. This is the 40 × `ggml_backend_synchronize()` barrier cost and is independent of hit-rate.

**100% hit-rate is physically impossible on this machine**: the model has 256 experts/layer × 3.23 MiB × 40 layers = **32.3 GB of expert weights**. Filling the slab to 100% hit-rate requires a 32+ GB dirty GPU buffer, which on a 48 GB machine with 17 GB of mmap model pages and OS overhead is infeasible. The 16 GB ceiling (the largest budget that stayed memory-safe here) only reaches 85.21% hit-rate.

**Even hypothetical 100% hit-rate ceiling**: eliminate all pread (0 ms/tok I/O) → decode wall = 27 ms/tok (compute/sync floor) → **~37 tok/s**. That is still only 0.68× of resident (54.7 tok/s), leaving a 1.48× remaining gap from the sync barriers alone.

---

### Verdict

**Enlarging the dirty GPU slab does not close the I/O gap safely or usefully.** 4× slab growth (4→16 GB) buys only +11% throughput (21.9→24.3 tok/s), the memory wall is not the failure mode (compressor stayed flat at ≤6.4 GB), but the physics are: dirty-slab DMA bandwidth degrades as the buffer grows, and the full 32.3 GB expert set cannot fit on this 48 GB machine. The zero-copy clean-page approach (MLX model, where the OS page cache itself serves as the "slab" at zero extra memory cost) is the only architecture that can reach high hit-rates without additional RAM or DMA bandwidth penalty. The zero-copy rewrite is **necessary, not avoidable** — and even then, the 27 ms/tok per-layer sync barrier remains as the next target (worth ~22% additional uplift if removed cleanly).</result>
